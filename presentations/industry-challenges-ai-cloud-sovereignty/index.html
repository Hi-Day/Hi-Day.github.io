Cloud trade-offs: cost, portability, reliability

Elasticity lowers the barrier to entry, but complexity can migrate from hardware ownership to service dependencies and operating costs.

＄

Cost visibility

GPU idle time, egress, overprovisioning, managed-service premiums, and unpredictable inference demand.

⇄

Portability

Provider-specific APIs, data gravity, migration effort, and differences in service semantics.

⟳

Reliability

Regional outages, capacity constraints, recovery objectives, and multi-cloud complexity.

07 · Security & trust

Trustworthy AI requires end-to-end assurance

Model & data risks

Data poisoning, leakage, prompt injection, unsafe tool use, and model misuse.

Infrastructure risks

Identity abuse, secrets exposure, tenant isolation failure, and software supply-chain compromise.

Operational risks

Untraceable decisions, evaluation drift, weak incident response, and unclear accountability.

08 · Sovereignty trade-offs

Sovereignty vs. global interoperability

Local control and global connectivity are not opposites; the challenge is to design explicit, testable boundaries.

⌂

Local control

Data location, access policy, auditability, jurisdictional obligations.

◎

Global ecosystem

Advanced services, research collaboration, scale, and access to innovation.

⚖

Risk of over-centralization

Single-provider dependency, limited substitutability, concentrated failure domains.

↗

Risk of over-isolation

Higher cost, reduced interoperability, duplicated effort, and slower innovation.

Design for controlled interdependence, not absolute self-sufficiency.
